NEW / WRIST–PALM ALIGNMENT / STATIC v3

A static landmark correction moves hand slots beyond the wrist housing.

Astro actual-hand assignments rise from 24–47% to 95–99% across both sources and centers. This is a controlled calibration demonstration, not a validated general repair. Raw learned Astro and G1 controls are preserved; motion retargeting remains on hold.

Why the housing attracts human hand slots

Height normalization leaves human wrists too proximal for Astro’s proportions. In height-normalized lateral coordinates, the human wrist is near 0.381 for SMPL-X and 0.408 for SOMA; Astro’s actual hand starts at 0.526, versus G1 at 0.416. Astro’s palm frame lies 159.5 mm beyond wrist-roll. The broad hand zone includes that housing, so its 99–100% score hid the mismatch.

The hand surfaces are sampled: Astro has 115/116 hand reference points, G1 92/92. Most wrist-bound slots have confident human hand labels. Full-triangle projection preserves the result. The inspected training loss rewards geometric coverage and smoothness, without a hand/wrist landmark constraint. These observations support a proportion and landmark mismatch; they do not isolate every source-pose or training effect.

One predeclared geometric correction, applied to Astro and G1

Use the 32 source slots nearest each elbow and wrist joint to estimate their learned output positions. Remap only the outward coordinate monotonically: keep the elbow and physical fingertip fixed, and send the learned wrist to the actual hand mesh start. Apply the same map to every slot in that interval, then project freely onto the whole visual model. Labels, other coordinates, source slots, target samples, model geometry and checkpoints stay fixed. No training was rerun.

The proximal hand mesh extent is an explicit operational anchor, not independently validated anatomical ground truth. The test intentionally moves the wrist towards the hand, so increased mesh membership alone is insufficient. Coverage reuses training samples; only one training seed and static pose are tested. Contacts, dynamics, articulation and motion remain untested.
