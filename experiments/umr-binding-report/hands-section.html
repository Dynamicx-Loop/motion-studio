A high hand-zone score is not a correct hand-mesh binding.

Only 24–47% of human hand slots land on the new same-side Dex3 palm or finger meshes, although broad hand-zone precision is 99–100%. Many remaining slots land on wrist-yaw housing. The broad geometric zone begins at the wrist plane; mesh identity is a stricter and separate diagnostic.

Fixed head: shared motion, potentially complementary constraints.

The user’s neck question concerns head binding. Both robots have no neck joint: Astro’s head and torso move with waist_roll_link; G1’s head mesh belongs directly to torso_link. Equal rigid ownership does not make separate surface residuals identical.

UMR’s default head selection requests 20 slots with point/normal costs 1/1, compared with 50 torso slots at 20/5. Its residuals use positions and normals at each selected surface location. Static probes found equal rotational Jacobians but different point Jacobians for head and torso. Additional head rows can change the weighted solution without adding an independent DOF or increasing rank.

Consolidating storage under one rigid owner can preserve the objective if coordinates, normals, geometry identity, labels, targets and weights are preserved. Relabeling head as torso changes default selection and costs; dropping head residuals changes task priority. Human neck motion may also conflict with the robot’s rigid head/torso assembly. These options need separate future ablations; no merge or correction was applied.
