03 / COMPLETE NUMERICAL INSPECTION

Follow each segment to its destination.

Explore all 18 common anatomical groups or the original 19 UMR labels. The common scheme separates head/face from neck and merges UMR’s artificial proximal-arm subdivision. It is a declared comparison vocabulary, not proof that 55-joint and 78-joint rigs are anatomically identical.

Definitions and edge cases
Zone precision
Compatible target-zone assignments divided by source slots in that region. Leakage is its complement.
Coverage within 20 mm
Fraction of expected-zone target reference samples within 20 mm of a projected slot from the source region. This reuses the training reference cloud; it is not held-out surface-area coverage.
High-confidence subset
At least 80% interpolated human LBS mass in the declared common group, and no mixed SOMA common-category triangle. A sensitivity check, not anatomical truth. It does not remove points from native totals.
Joint crossings
Axis-aligned canonical cuts: thigh points more than 20 mm below the knee, or forearm points more than 20 mm proximal to the elbow. Not bone-normal cuts or measured motion errors.
Mesh, body and moving owner
Mesh identifies visible geometry; MJCF body identifies attachment; moving owner is the first joint-bearing ancestor. G1’s head mesh belongs to torso_link. A body-name-only “head→torso” test would falsely flag it.
Neck, shoulder and feet
Neither Astro nor G1 has a neck DOF. The common neck compatibility convention allows head shell and upper torso. Clavicle surfaces can overlap trunk. Ankle shells straddle pivot planes, so strict foot zones can score poorly even on the correct foot mesh.

01 / THE MAIN TRADEOFF

Your centering observation holds—for specific metrics.

In the latest controlled experiment, spine1 means each source rig’s own Spine1 joint. bbox50 means the midpoint of its complete three-dimensional bounding box. Switching between them translates the same sampled source points; target meshes, target samples and training settings stay fixed. This is the strongest completed centering control.

Astro’s torso, hips and thigh precision generally improve with bbox50. G1’s corresponding metrics decline. Yet Astro forearm crossings increase, while G1 head precision remains 100%. A single “better / worse” score would hide those differences.

Open circle = spine1; filled circle = bbox50. Both measures are fractions, but have different denominators. These are descriptive audit conventions, not semantic ground-truth scores.

{{CENTER_TABLE}}

Astro: an improvement with residual failures

Head/face→torso falls from 70.8% to 43.1% for SMPL-X and 73.1% to 34.1% for SOMA. Thigh crossings more than 20 mm below the knee disappear for both sources.

Forearm proximal-elbow crossings increase: SMPL-X 43/42 → 49/44; SOMA 23/26 → 27/28 (L/R). Zero knee crossings also does not guarantee correct thigh distribution: SOMA bbox50 sends more thigh points toward hip/pelvis zones.

G1: different failure directions

Head/face precision stays at 100% in all four runs. Head-shell coverage falls 78.1% → 55.1% for SMPL-X and 83.2% → 56.9% for SOMA.

For SOMA, hips precision falls 74.5% → 7.3% and left/right thigh precision 92.3%/92.5% → 48.5%/46.4%. There are still zero below-knee crossings: leakage has moved toward other regions, rather than through that particular boundary.

Interpretation, not yet a causal result: origin alignment relative to each robot’s proportions is a plausible contributor. Astro retains a pelvis target origin; G1 retains a waist-yaw target origin. Neither “spine1” nor “bbox50” guarantees homologous source/target anatomy. The experiment changed the source origin only; it did not isolate target-origin effects.

Evidence: all common-region metrics, translation/equality controls, template geometry.

02 / SOURCE REPRESENTATION

SOMA changes the pattern; it does not remove the head mismatch.

HiPHI is a dataset. SMPL-X and SOMA are body representations. These experiments train on static human templates, not on the temporal frames of a motion. The SMPL-X template uses the HiPHI actor’s neutral model and ten shape coefficients. SOMA uses its public bind shape posed into the saved T-pose, not that same actor and not a zero-MHR forward mesh.

Sampling and selected-surface evidence

Independent full-triangle projection preserves the major findings

The latest audit reprojected all 32,768 robot slots using a complete triangle search. The production nearest-vertex candidate shortcut is locally approximate: 221–290 slots per arm improve by more than 1 µm; maximum distance improvements are 17.3–32.2 mm. Some body-owner changes arise from overlapping, equal-distance surfaces.

All eight head/face→torso counts and all forearm crossing counts remain unchanged. Only SOMA/Astro spine1 knee counts change from 77/62 to 78/61. This rules out the tested projection shortcut as the explanation for those major semantic mismatches. It does not make every production projection exact.

Evidence: geometry gates, complete-projection audit. Tables and viewers retain the original production projections; no result was silently replaced.

Working conclusion: the tested UMR workflow has configuration-sensitive anatomical correspondence limitations across embodiments. Astro is a severe case for several regions, not the only affected robot. The evidence does not establish a universal UMR failure, an intrinsic Astro defect, or a single repair.

Limits that must travel with the conclusion
  • One seed, one HiPHI actor and one public SOMA template. No confidence intervals or unseen-shape generalization.
  • Different source topology, proportions, pose and identity; source-model changes are not isolated representation effects.
  • spine1 is not the same physical point across rigs. bbox50 shifts all xyz components. Targets retain different origins.
  • 20 mm cuts, common groupings, 80% confidence and region boundaries are explicit diagnostic choices.
  • Coverage uses training reference samples, not independent held-out surface area. Perfect coverage can coexist with poor precision.
  • Welded body ownership, visible mesh identity and anatomical zone are not interchangeable.
  • Primitive and MimicKit fixes differ from the stock baseline; rejected primitive bindings are not accepted results.
  • Historical report wording that implied root-origin differences cancel under height normalization is superseded here: height normalization scales coordinates; it does not remove relative origin differences.
  • No motion/contact quality, foot sliding, joint limits, self-collision, dynamic feasibility or execution safety was evaluated.
